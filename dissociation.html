Calming Exercises — Dissociation

Come back to
yourself.

Dissociation can feel like fog, unreality, or watching yourself from a distance. These six techniques are designed specifically to pull you back into the present moment and your body.

About Dissociation
What is dissociation?

A disconnection between your thoughts, feelings, surroundings, or sense of identity. It's a protective response — your mind's way of managing overwhelming stress. It is not dangerous, and it passes.

Feeling detached from your body Things feeling unreal or dreamlike Time gaps or "zoning out" Emotional numbness Foggy or slow thoughts Feeling like a background character
If you're in distress

If dissociation feels severe, prolonged, or frightening — or you're struggling to stay safe — please reach out to a professional or helpline right away.

Technique 01 · Sensory Grounding
Name What You Notice

Technique 02 · Focused Attention
Study One Object

Pick any object near you. Spend 60–90 seconds investigating it like a detective. This intense, focused attention interrupts dissociative drift by giving your mind a concrete anchor.

Technique 03 · Physical Sensation
Anchor Through Your Body

Physical sensations are immediate and real — they override the disconnected feeling by reminding your nervous system that you have a body. Try one or more. Mark each done as you go.

Technique 04 · Spatial Orientation
Orient to Your Space

Answering simple factual questions about your immediate environment reactivates your sense of "where am I?" — one of the first things dissociation disrupts.

Technique 05 · Visualisation
Build Your Safe Place

Creating a detailed mental safe place gives your mind somewhere to go when reality feels overwhelming. The more sensory detail you build into it, the more effective it becomes as an anchor.

Your safe place

Edit safe place
Think of a real or imagined place where you feel completely safe and calm — a beach, a forest, a childhood room, anywhere. Describe it in as much sensory detail as you can.
Technique 06 · Breathwork
Physiological Sigh

A double inhale followed by a long exhale is the fastest known way to reduce acute stress and bring you back into your body. It works even mid-dissociation.

How it works: Short inhale through nose → short second inhale → long slow exhale through mouth. Repeat 3–5 times.
